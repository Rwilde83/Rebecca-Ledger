AI companies audit each other. Citizens audit the AI. And exposing corruption pays better than hiding it.

The fix

The gap on the ledger isn't a mystery and it isn't a math problem. It persists because nobody is required to close it and nobody is paid to expose it. Change both. Four layers, each one already proven somewhere, never yet assembled.

1

Three AI monitors watch each other and every large company

Three independent AI systems, run by organizations in different countries with different funding and different training. Each audits the other two, plus the payroll, executive pay, supplier contracts, and political spending of every company above a size threshold, in every country it sells in. Their auditing code is public. When they disagree, the disagreement is published too.

2

Citizens chosen by lottery check the AI

Panels of ordinary people selected at random from every participating country, not elected. Short terms, paid well, rotated constantly, identities protected while serving. They can demand plain-language explanations, order re-analysis, and escalate findings. The AI cannot overrule them.

3

Whoever exposes corruption gets paid

Anyone, anywhere, who reveals concealment or wrongdoing receives 20 to 40 percent of the penalty, funded from the penalty itself. The largest payouts go to exposing collusion among the monitors or inside a citizen panel, because those are the failures that would break the system. Legal protection and income replacement come with it.

The US SEC whistleblower program has paid out billions since 2011 for exactly this reason: it made reporting fraud more profitable than participating in it.

4

Humans judge. The AI only finds and publishes.

No monitor imposes a penalty. Findings that survive citizen review go to courts and public enforcement bodies in the relevant country. The AI is the auditor. It is never the judge.

This is also how you keep AI itself honest while it's still small enough to hold. An AI that audits companies in public, under citizen review, with its code open, is an AI that cannot quietly become something else. Build the watchdog before the thing it watches is too big to leash.

The rules it would enforce

The monitors enforce whatever the public sets. This is the starting proposal. It applies to every company above the size threshold, in every country, including their suppliers.

TierRule
FloorA local living wage in every country of operation, suppliers included. Not the legal minimum: enough for housing, food, healthcare, and savings where the person actually lives.
FrontlineLiving wage plus meaningful equity. Full-time available to everyone who wants it.
SkilledMarket pay. Bands published by level and location.
ExecutivesTotal compensation capped at 15 to 20 times the company's global median employee.
InnovationRoyalties on specific products, paid to the people who built them, capped per person per breakthrough. Reward invention where it happens, not two levels up.
Profit shareA fixed percentage of net income distributed to all employees.
TransparencyPay ratio and bands published every year, in plain language, in every language the company operates in.

Applied to Apple, the CEO goes from $74 million to about $3 million, every store worker gains $10 to $15k, every factory worker in the supply chain reaches a living wage, and the company stays the most profitable on earth. The cost fits inside one year of stock buybacks. The same arithmetic holds for nearly every company on the ledger.

Is anyone building this?

What exists

  • Companies use AI to audit themselves. The vendor works for the company.
  • Governments are regulating AI. They are not using it to audit corporations.
  • Whistleblower bounties exist and work at the SEC and a few other agencies.
  • Citizens' assemblies have run successfully in Ireland, France, and dozens of cities.
  • Pay ratios are public by law in the US and UK, buried in filings almost no one reads.

What is missing

  • A publicly owned AI that audits companies rather than serving them.
  • Any arrangement where AI labs monitor each other.
  • A connection between the bounty and the detector.
  • Anyone with the incentive to build it. The labs sell compliance tools to the companies they would be auditing.

Every component is proven. The assembly is unbuilt. That is either a reason it can't work, or a reason it hasn't been tried. Help figure out which.

Known weak points, so you don't have to find them
  • All three major AI labs today share a country, investors, and a hiring pool. That is one faction, not three.
  • Correlated blind spots: AIs trained on similar data miss the same things. The citizen panels exist to catch this, and might not.
  • The system guarantees compliance, not fairness. If people agree on bad rules, it will enforce bad rules.
  • Capital can flow to countries that opt out. The only lever is market access: sell here, get audited here.
  • Public ownership only works if the public watches. Apathy beats every other failure mode.
